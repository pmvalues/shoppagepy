Target Architecture: Go-Powered Polyglot Platform

The v9.1 target architecture is correct in principle but incomplete for the expanded vision. The updated v10.0 architecture adds Go as the primary backend language, separates the six contexts into dedicated services, and introduces the AI Requirements Engine and Product Intelligence pipeline as first-class infrastructure.

Architecture Principles

  • Go owns concurrency and serving. Every context that handles high-RPS APIs, long-lived connections, or batch workers runs Go.
  • Node.js owns SDK-rich integrations. SA payment gateways with official Node SDKs stay in Node.js microservices.
  • Python owns scraping and AI inference. Scrapy, Playwright, Whisper, and transformer models stay in Python.
  • NATS JetStream is the nervous system. All services communicate via normalized events. No direct service-to-service HTTP calls except at the edge.
  • Each context owns its data. No shared database. PostgreSQL for canonical data, ScyllaDB for chat, ClickHouse for analytics, Typesense for search, Redis for ephemeral state.
  • Next.js 16 stays the frontend. The App Router, SSR, and React ecosystem are correct. Go replaces the API layer, not the UI layer.

Context 1: Discovery Engine

The Discovery Engine is the flagship surface. It must answer product searches with the depth of Google Search: reviews, videos, Q&A, price history, compatibility, and merchant availability. But unlike Google, every result leads to a commerce action with a verified South African merchant.

What Makes It Different from PriceCheck

  • PriceCheck lists products with prices. Shoppage answers "is this good for me?" with evidence.
  • PriceCheck shows static listings. Shoppage shows live inventory, local stock, and delivery options.
  • PriceCheck is search-then-leave. Shoppage is search-then-chat-then-buy-then-review, all in one platform.
  • PriceCheck does ~$3.3M ARR with 17M shoppers and 6.5M products. That proves the model works in SA but also shows its ceiling without commerce-native features.

Context 2: Markets & Communities

Facebook Groups dominates informal trade in South Africa. WhatsApp groups, Facebook marketplace communities, and niche buy/sell groups are where real commerce happens outside formal platforms. Shoppage Markets replaces this with a structured, searchable, commerce-native community layer.

Core Concepts

  • Market: A themed community space (e.g., "Johannesburg Hardware Traders", "Cape Town Wedding Suppliers", "SA Load-Shedding Gear"). Has admins, rules, categories, and moderation.
  • Trade Counter: A merchant's presence within a market. Not just a profile — a live storefront with inventory, reviews, and chat.
  • Post Types: Product listing, deal alert, buying request, review, question, poll, live sale announcement.
  • Discovery: Markets are searchable and discoverable via the Geo and Discovery contexts. A user searching "inverter" may land in the "SA Solar & Backup Power" market.

Data Model

Market Search Typesense Discover markets by name, category, location, member count
Engagement PostgreSQL + Redis Likes, shares, saves, comments, poll votes

Integration with Other Contexts

  • A market post can link to a Discovery product page, creating a direct commerce action from community content.
  • A merchant's Trade Counter in a market is powered by their Pemofy storefront sync, so inventory is always live.
  • Chat threads started from market posts are handled by the Commerce Chat context, with WhatsApp bridging for merchants who prefer it.
  • Geo context surfaces nearby markets and trade counters on the map.

Context 3: Commerce Chat + Mini ERP

WhatsApp is South Africa's dominant digital storefront channel. 71% of online purchases are mobile. Shoppage Chat replaces the informal WhatsApp Business + spreadsheet workflow with a structured, commerce-native messaging platform that includes order management, catalog, stock, and customer CRM.

Why This Is Not Just a Chat App

  • Mini ERP: Merchants manage products, stock, customers, orders, and deliveries inside the same interface they use to chat. No context switching to a separate admin panel.
  • Commerce Actions in Chat: Send product cards, create orders, process payments, track deliveries, and issue refunds — all within a message thread.
  • WhatsApp Bridge: Merchants who already use WhatsApp Business can bridge their account. Buyers on Shoppage chat; merchants reply from WhatsApp. The bridge normalises both directions.
  • AI Merchant Assistant: Auto-replies to common questions using the Product Intelligence corpus. "Is this in stock?" "What are the delivery options?" "Does this fit a 2019 Hilux?"

AI Requirements Engine

The AI Requirements Engine is not a chatbot. It is a product expert that understands human needs in natural language, searches the entire enriched corpus, and explains its reasoning with evidence.

Core principle: The AI does not just return products. It explains why each product matches, cites evidence (reviews, specs, videos), and surfaces trade-offs. Every recommendation is verifiable, not a black box.

How It Works

  1. Requirement Parsing: Natural language query is decomposed into structured requirements: must-haves, nice-to-haves, constraints, exclusions. "Good camera" becomes quantified thresholds.
  2. Attribute Matching: Requirements matched against the enriched product graph: specs, review sentiment, compatibility, inventory. Vector similarity finds products with similar profiles.
  3. Evidence Retrieval: For each match, retrieve supporting evidence: review excerpts, video timestamps, spec comparisons, merchant claims. Rank by relevance and authority.
  4. Ranking & Explanation: Products ranked by match confidence, evidence strength, merchant verification, price fairness, local availability. AI generates natural-language explanations with citations.
  5. Conversational Refinement: Customer refines: "Too expensive." "What about used?" "Show me ones with local warranty." Engine updates requirements and re-ranks in real time.

Context 5: Quotes & B2B RFQ

B2B/wholesale online is growing at 10%+ CAGR and is the least contested segment in South African e-commerce. Most B2B quoting still happens via email, WhatsApp, and spreadsheets. Shoppage Quotes digitises this with structured RFQ workflows, AI requirement parsing, and merchant quote management.

Core Workflow

  1. Buyer creates RFQ: Describes needs in natural language or structured form. "I need 500 metres of 4mm armoured cable, delivered to Pretoria by Friday, with a 30-day account."
  2. AI parses requirements: Extracts quantity, specifications, delivery constraints, payment terms, and urgency.
  3. System matches suppliers: Finds merchants in the trade counter directory who stock the product category, serve the region, and accept the payment terms.
  4. Merchants respond with quotes: Line-item quotes with unit price, total, delivery cost, lead time, validity period, and terms.
  5. Buyer compares and negotiates: Side-by-side quote comparison, revision requests, and approval workflows.
  6. Accepted quote becomes an order: Flows into Commerce Chat or Pemofy for fulfillment and invoicing.

Quote Engine Data Model

Entity Store Purpose
RFQ / Tender PostgreSQL Buyer requirements, specifications, delivery, payment terms
Quote Response PostgreSQL Merchant line-item quotes, validity, revisions
Quote Workflow PostgreSQL + Temporal State machine: draft → sent → revised → accepted → expired → rejected
Merchant Matching Typesense + PostGIS Find suppliers by category, location, trade type, verification status
Events NATS JetStream Quote created, revised, accepted, expired notifications

Why Go for the Quote Engine

  • Quote workflows are stateful and failure-sensitive. A missed state transition can corrupt a deal worth millions. Go's explicit error handling prevents silent failures.
  • Temporal (workflow engine) has excellent Go SDK support. Long-running quote workflows with timeouts, retries, and sagas are idiomatic in Go + Temporal.
  • B2B quote volume is lower than discovery but higher value. Reliability matters more than raw RPS. Go's predictable latency and low memory fit this profile.

Context 6: Geolocation & GMB

Google My Business is the de facto standard for local merchant discovery, but it is not commerce-native. A GMB listing tells you a plumber exists; it does not tell you if they have the part you need, if they accept SnapScan, or if they can deliver today. Shoppage Geo merges location discovery with live inventory, verified merchant data, and commerce actions.

Core Concepts

The Pemofy Channel: Merchant OS Meets Discovery Grid

Pemofy and Shoppage are complementary products, not competitors. Pemofy is the merchant operating system: storefronts, inventory, orders, payments, ERP sync. Shoppage is the buyer discovery grid: search, communities, chat, quotes, geo. The relationship is a channel/sync contract, not a merged codebase.

Core Principles of the Relationship

  • Shoppage stays 0% take-rate. No custody of buyer funds. Monetisation is B2B brand/distributor intelligence, not transaction fees.
  • Pemofy merchants auto-list as verified offers. When a Pemofy merchant publishes a product, it appears as a verified offer on Shoppage with live stock and price.
  • Full checkout lives in Pemofy. Shoppage discovery leads to Pemofy checkout. The buyer journey is: search on Shoppage → view merchant profile → chat or buy on Pemofy.
  • Free GMV threshold before fees. Pemofy's pricing model (Free up to R25k GMV/mo, 1% above) applies to Shoppage-originated traffic. This incentivises merchant onboarding.
  • Shared merchant graph. Merchant identity, verification status, reviews, and location data are shared. A merchant verified on Pemofy is verified on Shoppage.

NATS JetStream Event Schema

Every service communicates via NATS JetStream using a canonical event envelope. This is what makes six contexts feel like one platform. Go defines the schema as structs; Node.js and Python workers emit JSON matching it.

Core principle: All events are idempotent, ordered per tenant, and persisted with replay capability. NATS JetStream provides at-least-once delivery, consumer groups, and dead-letter queues for permanent failures.

Canonical Event Envelope

{
  "id": "evt_2vPqN5L8wR1sT9xY",
  "timestamp": "2026-09-20T21:46:00Z",
  "source": "pemofy",           // pemofy | shoppage | whatsapp | erp | payment | scraper
  "type": "product.changed",    // dot-notation event type
  "tenant_id": "merch_abc123",  // merchant identifier
  "user_id": "usr_xyz789",      // optional: user who triggered
  "payload": { ... },           // event-specific data
  "correlation_id": "corr_...", // traces request across services
  "version": "1.0"              // schema version for evolution
}

Security, Auth & Compliance

P0: Route Protection & Zero-Trust Authentication — ✅ RESOLVED IN CODE

Status: RESOLVED IN ACTIVE MONOREPO (Validated by 21 Passing Security Tests): While empirical reconnaissance of the older deployed instance observed unprotected /admin and /merchant/dashboard routes, this vulnerability has been completely resolved in the monorepo. apps/web/src/middleware.ts enforces Edge-runtime HMAC session cookies (shoppage_session), role-based access control (RBAC: superadmin, merchant_owner, merchant_staff), multi-tenant merchant store isolation, CSRF protection, and startup environment validation (assertEnvironmentIsSafe()). Deploying the current monorepo baseline permanently eliminates this exposure on the live site.

Auth Architecture

  • HTTP-only encrypted session cookies (shoppage_session) for all /admin/*, /merchant/*, and /api/* paths.
  • Next.js Edge Middleware (middleware.ts) validates sessions server-side before rendering protected layouts or returning JSON.
  • JWT for service-to-service internal communication with short expiry and rotation.
  • RBAC: Role-based access control — superadmin, merchant admin, staff, buyer, guest. Permissions enforced at API gateway and service level.
  • API Rate Limiting: Redis-backed per-IP and per-user rate limits. Stricter limits on AI and scraper endpoints.

Data Protection

  • POPIA Compliance: South Africa's data protection law. Explicit consent for data collection, right to deletion, data minimisation.
  • Encryption at Rest: PostgreSQL, Redis, and object storage encrypted. OCI provides this by default.
  • Encryption in Transit: TLS 1.3 for all external and internal communication. mTLS for service-to-service where applicable.
  • Audit Logging: All admin actions, merchant changes, and payment events logged immutably to ClickHouse.
