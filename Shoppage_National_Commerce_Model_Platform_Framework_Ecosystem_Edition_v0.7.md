# Document purpose and decision status

This document replaces the earlier Shopscene / ShopPage naming structure and extends the Shoppage implementation blueprint. The whole project is Shoppage. Shoppage Central, Showcase, Ads, Creators and Markets are connected operating layers under one identity system, commerce graph, trust framework, data model and commercial strategy. This edition adds the non-negotiable defensibility, intelligence, international-quality, AI, daily-utility and investor requirements needed to build a national platform rather than a copyable marketplace. It also integrates the validated Shoppage Commerce Intelligence Foundation v0.4 and the implementation progress report dated 10 July 2026, distinguishing technical proof, discovery candidates, verified claims and production commercial truth. Version 0.7 further establishes Shoppage as a reusable commerce operating framework for first-party products, subsidiaries, certified plugins, powered partners and white-label deployments while keeping identity, evidence, trust and intelligence unified.

# 1. Executive decision

Shoppage should be built as the local operating and distribution system for Zimbabwean commerce. The platform must organise the commercial activity that is currently fragmented across Facebook pages and groups, Facebook Marketplace, WhatsApp catalogues and statuses, Google Search and Maps, short-form video, classifieds, physical markets, shopping centres, informal sellers, creators and diaspora buying networks.

The strongest strategic position is not to ask Zimbabweans to abandon Facebook or WhatsApp immediately. Shoppage should first make those channels work better by giving every merchant a structured catalogue, trackable links, shoppable videos, verified identity, fresh stock signals and measurable leads. It then progressively converts external reach into Shoppage-owned demand, data and advertising inventory.

The business succeeds when a buyer thinks "search Shoppage" before asking a Facebook group, scrolling old posts or calling multiple shops. It succeeds commercially when a merchant allocates part of the budget previously spent on Facebook boosts, Google ads, page administrators or informal influencers to Shoppage because the platform produces clearer local buyer intent and attributable outcomes.

The explicit strategic objective is to substitute a material share of commercial product discovery and performance advertising that currently flows through Google and Meta-controlled surfaces. This does not mean blocking, attacking or depending on regulation against those platforms. It means building a Zimbabwean product-search, commerce-media and advertising system that merchants and buyers voluntarily prefer because it contains fresher local inventory, better location intelligence, clearer trust signals, lower campaign complexity and more measurable local outcomes. Shoppage must track this objective through commercial-discovery share, merchant advertising-wallet share and local value-retention metrics rather than slogans.

Implementation has now begun at the intelligence-foundation level. The validated package contains 6,101 organisation-source records, one million unique global food product identities, a 25,210-node Zimbabwe place and market scaffold, and a tested merchant-offer ingestion contract. These figures are foundation milestones, not claims of complete Zimbabwe commercial coverage. The immediate priority is to convert the foundation into rights-cleared, fresh local offers and verified organisation, branch and market relationships in the launch wedge.


## 1.1 What "fully functional in three months" means

A fully functional version 1 means every layer can complete its primary commercial job end to end. It does not mean every long-term feature is present. The scope boundary is essential because AI-assisted development accelerates implementation, but it does not remove the need for product decisions, data quality, security, merchant onboarding, moderation, field operations and adoption.


# 2. Zimbabwe ground reality and design consequences

The model must work with actual Zimbabwean constraints rather than imported assumptions. Public evidence published in 2025-2026 describes e-commerce as largely informal, urban-centred and diaspora-driven, with Facebook Marketplace and WhatsApp central to discovery and negotiation. Cash, cash on delivery, mobile payments and direct arrangements remain more common than conventional card-first e-commerce. Internet reach is material but not universal, and data cost, trust, fragmented logistics and uneven digital capability remain binding constraints.

Digital 2026 estimates indicate approximately 6.54 million individual internet users and 2.60 million social media user identities in Zimbabwe at the end of 2025, alongside 16.2 million active cellular connections. These numbers justify a mobile-first opportunity, but they also warn against designing for permanent high-speed connectivity or assuming every mobile connection represents an active internet shopper.

The correct product stance is therefore hybrid: smartphone-first but low-data; digital-first but assisted; national in ambition but city-by-city in execution; payments-enabled but not checkout-dependent; content-rich but linked to structured products; and commercially independent while partnership-ready for government, financial institutions, mobile operators, brands and local authorities.


# 3. National value proposition and endorsement readiness

Presidential-level or national endorsement cannot be guaranteed and should never be treated as a substitute for product-market fit. Shoppage can, however, be designed to be endorsement-ready by producing measurable public value, maintaining political neutrality, complying with law and presenting a credible national-development case.


## 3.1 Presidential and institutional briefing package

- A two-page national impact brief with quantified jobs, MSME digitisation, local ad retention and provincial coverage.

- A live demonstration showing a market trader creating a catalogue, a buyer finding a nearby product and a creator earning from a tracked lead.

- A 100-day scorecard with independently verifiable figures rather than vanity registrations.

- A data-governance note explaining privacy, lawful access, localisation roadmap and non-partisan controls.

- A partnership menu for ministries, local authorities, ZimTrade, financial institutions, telecom operators, market associations and universities.

- A clear statement that endorsement does not confer ownership, exclusive access or procurement preference.


# 4. Brand and product architecture

Shoppage is the master brand. The five layers should appear as connected destinations and capabilities, not separate companies. Users may enter through any layer, but identity, catalogue, trust, analytics, billing and data remain unified.

Recommended domain pattern: shoppage.co.zw as the public destination; manage.shoppage.co.zw for merchant operations; ads.shoppage.co.zw for campaign management; creators.shoppage.co.zw for creator tools; and market pages under shoppage.co.zw/markets/{slug}. During the first three months these may be routes in one application rather than separate deployments. This reduces cost and prevents duplicated authentication, analytics and user experience.

The name should undergo trademark, company-name and domain due diligence before major public investment. Brand guidelines should establish one logo system, one typography and colour system, one trust badge hierarchy and one naming rule. "Shoppage" should always refer to the ecosystem; module names are descriptors, not independent brands.


# 5. Shoppage Central - merchant operating system

Shoppage Central is the foundation. It must create standalone value before Shoppage has national consumer traffic. A merchant should gain an organised digital presence, a catalogue, social-ready content, lead tracking and basic business insight even when most customers still arrive from Facebook or WhatsApp.


## 5.1 Core merchant jobs

- Create and verify a business, seller or market-stall identity.

- Create one or more stores, branches, pickup points and delivery zones.

- Add products through manual entry, spreadsheet, assisted upload, image extraction or approved import.

- Manage price, currency, price validity, promotions, stock state and last-confirmed availability.

- Upload product images and short videos, then reuse them across Showcase and external social channels.

- Receive leads from WhatsApp, calls, quote requests, reservations and creator content.

- Assign staff roles and track who changed prices, stock or campaign settings.

- View actionable analytics rather than only impressions and likes.

- Buy advertising, invite creators and join relevant Shoppage Markets.


## 5.2 Merchant experience design

The merchant interface must be mobile-first. Refine or another dense admin framework can remain useful for Shoppage operations and larger merchants, but the typical vendor experience should be a simplified Next.js mobile workspace. The most common actions - confirm stock, change price, upload a clip, answer a lead and boost a product - should require no more than a few taps.


## 5.3 Freshness as a product system

Listing freshness is a core commercial control, not a cosmetic timestamp. Each sellable item needs a freshness state computed from merchant confirmation, price changes, enquiries, stock updates and customer reports.

Category windows should differ. Fast-moving electronics and groceries need shorter confirmation intervals than custom furniture or building services. Merchants must be able to bulk-confirm unchanged items, while high-risk mass confirmation should trigger spot checks.


# 6. Shoppage Showcase - geolocal discovery and commerce video

Showcase is the buyer front door. It combines high-intent search with a low-data short-video commerce feed. It should not imitate TikTok as a general entertainment network. Its advantage is that every useful piece of content resolves into a product, vendor, market, offer, service or buyer action.


## 6.1 Core buyer surfaces


## 6.2 Geolocal commerce engine

Geo should influence every major ranking and advertising decision. The system must recognise country, province, city, suburb, commercial cluster, branch, pickup point, delivery radius and market boundary. Location must never be inferred more precisely than necessary without user permission.

- Products near me, available today and open now.

- Deals within a selected distance or delivery zone.

- Branch-level availability and collection options.

- Popular products in a suburb, city or market cluster.

- Buyer requests routed by category, radius, trust and response capacity.

- Ads constrained by service area so merchants do not pay for unreachable demand.

- Geo-demand reports that help vendors choose stock, branches and campaigns.

Manual location selection is mandatory because GPS may be disabled, inaccurate or costly. Users should be able to choose a city, suburb, landmark or market. Rural and peri-urban discovery should use towns, growth points, routes and delivery corridors rather than assuming street-level addressing.


## 6.3 Short-form commerce video

For version 1, clips should normally be 10-60 seconds, compressed and auto-captioned. Autoplay should be muted, preloading conservative and a low-data mode available. A managed video service should handle direct creator uploads, encoding, adaptive delivery, thumbnails and basic analytics. Cloudflare Stream is a practical initial choice; Mux is a credible alternative if its analytics or workflow fit is superior.


# 7. Shoppage Ads - Zimbabwean commercial attention and measurable demand

Shoppage Ads is the primary strategic mechanism for retaining more advertising value inside Zimbabwe. It must not copy the full complexity of Meta Ads Manager. The initial product should be simpler, locally understandable and focused on commercial outcomes that merchants can verify. Its formal target is to become the default performance-marketing channel for local product availability, nearby services, market traffic, creator commerce and buyer requests in the categories and cities Shoppage covers. Google and Meta remain distribution partners during the transition, but Shoppage must progressively own the product graph, buyer intent, campaign measurement, merchant relationship and repeat audience.


## 7.1 Advertising doctrine

- Sell purchase intent, qualified reach and measurable action - not vanity engagement.

- Only promote products or services with adequate identity, content and freshness quality.

- Label every paid placement clearly while preserving useful organic results.

- Give small merchants simple objectives and guardrails; give agencies and brands deeper controls later.

- Keep billing transparent across USD and ZWG display, with dated exchange assumptions.

- Prevent political advertising from entering the commerce system during the initial operating period.

- Protect buyers from misleading discounts, counterfeit claims and unsafe products.


## 7.2 Version 1 ad products


## 7.3 Campaign workflow

1. Choose an objective: visibility, catalogue visits, WhatsApp leads, calls, quote requests, store visits or sales later.

1. Select product, store, market or video creative.

1. Select category, city/area, budget, dates and optional buyer signals.

1. Run automated checks for prohibited content, stale inventory, missing price, weak media and misleading claims.

1. Approve automatically for low-risk trusted advertisers or route to human review.

1. Deliver placements with frequency limits and organic-result safeguards.

1. Report spend, reach, clicks, leads, response and verified outcomes.

1. Provide AI-generated recommendations without silently changing budget or targeting.

Merchants with limited digital skill should be able to buy a fixed package such as "Promote this product in Harare for US$20 over seven days". Larger merchants need account billing, campaign groups, branches, product feeds and downloadable reports. Managed campaigns should be a first-class service because they create early revenue while the self-service market matures.


## 7.4 Local advertising retention model

Shoppage cannot claim that every advertising dollar remains in Zimbabwe because hosting, payment processing, software and international infrastructure may create foreign costs. The credible claim is that platform revenue, merchant relationships, campaign capability, creator payouts, employment and commerce data are governed through a Zimbabwean business rather than being fully captured by global platforms.


# 8. Shoppage Creators - affiliate commerce and content production

Creators are not an optional marketing add-on. They are a distributed sales, education and merchant-digitisation network. Zimbabwean Facebook group administrators, WhatsApp community operators, videographers, product reviewers, campus promoters, technicians and local deal pages can all become commerce creators.


## 8.1 Creator roles


## 8.2 Creator safeguards

- Every paid or commission-bearing post must carry a standard sponsored or affiliate disclosure.

- Creators cannot publish fabricated reviews, false scarcity, undisclosed ownership or unsupported performance claims.

- High-risk categories require subject-matter review or may be excluded.

- Attribution must resist self-referrals, fake clicks, duplicate leads and collusion with vendors.

- Payouts should depend on a documented event definition and a dispute window.

- Creators need content rights, music rights, customer-consent and privacy training.

- Vendors must approve products and commission terms before creator promotion.


## 8.3 Attribution model

Version 1 attribution should be simple and auditable. Each creator receives a tagged product or collection link and optional campaign code. The platform records view, product open, save, WhatsApp click, call, quote request and vendor-confirmed outcome. Commission should initially favour verified leads and fixed content fees because most purchases will close outside Shoppage.


# 9. Shoppage Markets - market-in-market infrastructure

Zimbabwean commerce is clustered in malls, CBD blocks, industrial areas, flea markets, growth points, supplier streets, agricultural centres and informal trading spaces. Shoppage Markets digitises the cluster itself, creating a unit of discovery and advertising above the individual shop.


## 9.1 Market types

- Physical malls and shopping centres.

- CBD blocks, streets and arcades.

- Flea markets, market stalls and informal trader associations.

- Industrial and supplier clusters such as hardware, automotive or manufacturing zones.

- Thematic online markets such as Home and Power, Back to School or Made in Zimbabwe.

- Temporary event markets, expos, agricultural shows and seasonal campaigns.

- Diaspora-curated markets for home building, gifting, school and family support.


## 9.2 Market operating model


## 9.3 Market page requirements

- Market identity, location, boundaries, hours, access and contact.

- Vendor directory with categories, verification and branch or stall position.

- Combined product and short-video feed.

- Market-wide search and buyer request routing.

- Events, campaigns, seasonal promotions and sponsorship placements.

- QR signage that opens a specific market, vendor, aisle or collection.

- Market analytics: demand, vendor activity, searches, leads and campaign results.

- Admin controls for membership, content rules and complaints.

Markets create a practical field distribution model. Instead of acquiring vendors one by one across a city, Shoppage can sign a market owner or association, train one captain and activate a dense supply cluster. Dense clusters improve buyer utility, creator content and local advertising inventory simultaneously.

The five layers depend on common engines. Building them once prevents fragmented user accounts, inconsistent data and duplicated costs.


# 11. Priority users and jobs to be done

# 12. End-to-end commercial journeys

The business model should be tested through complete journeys, not isolated features.


## 12.1 Merchant activation journey

1. Merchant is recruited through field agent, market captain, creator, referral or self-registration.

1. Phone and identity are verified; business and location evidence are added progressively.

1. AI-assisted onboarding extracts products from approved photos, spreadsheets or merchant-provided social material.

1. Merchant confirms title, price, currency, stock, location and fulfilment.

1. Shoppage creates the storefront, QR code and shareable product links.

1. Merchant publishes a clip or exports a social post that links back to the structured catalogue.

1. First lead is tracked; merchant receives a response reminder and closes the lead.

1. Merchant is offered a small ad or creator campaign only after catalogue quality is adequate.


## 12.2 Buyer journey

1. Buyer arrives from search, shared link, market QR, creator clip or direct visit.

1. Buyer selects location or grants coarse location permission.

1. Buyer searches, browses nearby, watches clips or submits a structured request.

1. Results show relevance, distance, freshness, trust and sponsored status.

1. Buyer opens product, checks seller, price, branch and fulfilment.

1. Buyer saves, compares, calls, WhatsApps or requests a quote without mandatory registration.

1. Shoppage records the lead source and prompts the merchant to respond.

1. Buyer can report inaccurate stock, misleading price or seller conduct.


## 12.3 Creator campaign journey

1. Vendor selects approved products, creator eligibility and commission or production budget.

1. Creators apply or are invited based on category, area and quality.

1. Brief, disclosure, content rights, dates and payout events are agreed.

1. Creator records a tagged clip or collection and submits for review.

1. Content publishes in Showcase and may be exported externally.

1. Attribution follows view-to-action events and verified outcomes.

1. Vendor and creator receive transparent reporting; disputes follow a defined process.

Shoppage will face global platforms with enormous reach and local platforms with existing awareness. It should not attempt to beat each player at its strongest feature. It should combine local functions that no single competitor currently integrates well.

# 14. Network effects and defensible moat

The interface can be copied. The defensible asset is the interconnected local commerce graph and the operating relationships required to keep it accurate. The v0.4 implementation package establishes the first reproducible version of that graph through source records, evidence states, classification, matching queues, product masters, place nodes and offer observations. The moat now depends on converting this seed into fresh Zimbabwe commercial truth without overstating discovery data.

The strongest flywheel is: merchants create fresh supply; supply makes Showcase useful; buyer actions reveal demand; demand attracts advertisers and creators; ads and creators generate merchant outcomes; outcomes improve retention and fund more onboarding; markets create dense clusters that accelerate every step.


# 15. Revenue model and pricing architecture

Shoppage must diversify revenue while keeping merchant entry friction low. Subscription revenue alone will be insufficient early because many MSMEs need proof of leads before committing to recurring software fees. Managed services, advertising, onboarding and institutional packages should support the first six months.


## 15.1 Revenue pillars


## 15.2 Pricing hypotheses for validation

Pricing is a test framework, not a final tariff. Plans must be configurable and allow local invoices in supported currencies. Shoppage should avoid hard-coding product limits, fees or commission rates. Early founding merchants may receive time-limited free Growth access in exchange for complete catalogues, weekly feedback and permission to use performance case studies.


## 15.3 Scenario economics - not a forecast

The revenue ranges depend more on field activation, ad sales and merchant retention than on software completion. Break-even should not be assumed by month six. The base case requires disciplined costs, founder-led sales, local talent, managed infrastructure and avoidance of a proprietary logistics fleet.


# 16. Delivery budget and operating model

AI-assisted development can compress coding time, but a credible launch still requires engineering, product, design, field operations, merchant success, moderation, sales and compliance. The budget should be chosen explicitly rather than allowing an undefined scope to consume cash.

These ranges exclude large subsidies, major celebrity campaigns, owned delivery vehicles, office build-outs and national advertising. Cloud, video and messaging costs should start modestly but be instrumented by user and feature so that unit economics remain visible.

# 18. Field operations and the 1,000-entity market register

The 1,000-entity research requirement should become an operational asset rather than a presentation claim. Shoppage should build a structured market register during launch, combining research, sales pipeline and catalogue acquisition.

Each record must have a source, date, owner, consent status where contact data is stored, verification state and next action. Public Facebook observations can inform patterns, but Shoppage must not violate platform terms, bypass access controls or scrape private data. Merchant-authorised imports and manual field verification are the safe default.


## 18.1 Merchant activation standard


# 19. Trust, safety and consumer protection

Trust should be visible, progressive and explainable. Shoppage must not create a badge that buyers interpret as a guarantee when only a phone number was checked.


## 19.1 Safety controls

- Prohibited and restricted goods policy before launch.

- High-risk category rules and manual review.

- Suspicious price and duplicate-image detection.

- New-account limits on ads, reach and mass uploads.

- Buyer warnings for off-platform deposits and unverifiable claims.

- Complaint intake, evidence upload, response deadlines and appeal.

- Creator and advertiser disclosure enforcement.

- Product recall and urgent content takedown process.

- Law-enforcement request process reviewed by counsel and logged.

Reviews should not launch as an unmoderated star-rating system. Early trust is better built from verified transactions or leads, response metrics, listing accuracy, complaint handling and structured buyer feedback. Anonymous public accusations can create defamation and manipulation risk.


# 20. Payments, currencies, fulfilment and logistics

The architectural boundary is now explicit: Shoppage owns the transaction and fulfilment workflow, evidence and customer experience, while licensed payment providers and approved merchant or logistics partners execute regulated money movement and physical delivery. This is on-platform orchestration, not an uncontrolled off-platform handoff.

Shoppage must create and retain the commercial record, payment status, fulfilment choice, proof, complaint and outcome. It should not initially hold customer funds, store sensitive payment credentials, operate a wallet, pool multi-vendor settlements, own a fleet or run warehouses. The merchant remains the merchant of record for normal vendor sales.

Multi-vendor settlement, Shoppage-held escrow, universal refunds and chargebacks, owned fulfilment and warehousing are intentionally excluded from the first six months. Part V defines the payment, fulfilment, Merchant Site and Pemosites decisions in implementation detail.


# 21. AI operating model

AI should reduce the cost of creating and maintaining a high-quality local commerce graph. It should not be positioned as a decorative chatbot or allowed to make unreviewed commercial, moderation or payment decisions.


## 21.1 AI for merchant operations

- Extract product titles, prices, attributes and contacts from merchant-authorised images, PDFs and spreadsheets.

- Clean titles, standardise brands and suggest categories while preserving original data.

- Generate missing descriptions, specifications and social captions for merchant approval.

- Detect duplicates, improbable prices, stale products and conflicting branch information.

- Create video captions, thumbnails, price overlays and translated text.

- Summarise leads and suggest follow-up actions.

- Recommend which products need confirmation, promotion or better media.


## 21.2 AI for buyers, ads and creators

- Semantic search across local naming variations and mixed-language queries.

- Buyer-request parsing and vendor matching.

- Product and content recommendations constrained by location, freshness and safety.

- Campaign budget and creative suggestions based on verified outcomes.

- Creator-product matching based on category, geography, quality and audience fit.

- Fraud, spam, prohibited-content and misleading-claim risk scoring.

- Customer-support triage with human escalation.

# 22. Technology architecture

The existing modular-monolith direction remains appropriate. The launch architecture should maximise speed, type safety, tenant isolation and managed infrastructure while keeping future extraction possible. The commerce-intelligence package uses SQLite for reproducible scale testing and package delivery; production authority must remain PostgreSQL, with immutable source evidence, review services, current-state materialisations and search indexes connected through NestJS.

A separate native mobile app is not required for the 0-3 month launch. A strong PWA provides shareable URLs, search visibility, app-like navigation and one codebase. Native applications can be introduced after repeat usage, notification needs, offline workflows or device integration justify the maintenance cost.


## 22.1 Module boundaries


# 23. Data governance, privacy and security

Shoppage will process identity, location, contact, behavioural, advertising and potentially payment-related data. Privacy and security must be designed before launch, not added after an endorsement or incident. Data governance must also preserve predicate-specific source authority, licensing, observation time, evidence hashes, confidence, review state and unresolved conflicts. A public record is not automatically reusable content or verified current truth.

- Register and operate as required under Zimbabwe data-protection rules, with counsel confirming controller and processor obligations.

- Collect the minimum location precision and personal data needed for each function.

- Use explicit purpose notices for merchant verification, buyer accounts, creator attribution and advertising.

- Maintain consent, lawful-basis, access, correction, deletion and retention workflows.

- Separate public business data from private identity and verification documents.

- Encrypt data in transit and at rest; protect secrets with managed key and environment controls.

- Apply PostgreSQL row-level security as defence in depth, but keep NestJS authorisation authoritative.

- Require multi-factor authentication for platform administrators and sensitive merchant roles.

- Log administrative access, verification decisions, campaign edits and data exports.

- Prepare breach response, vendor-risk review, backup restore tests and incident communications.

Data sovereignty should be a staged operational commitment. Version 1 may rely on reputable global managed infrastructure because reliability and security matter. Shoppage should maintain data portability, local corporate control, documented sub-processors, regional backup strategy and a roadmap for increased local or regional hosting where technically and economically justified.

# 26. 4-6 month expansion roadmap

Months four to six expand a working system. They should not be used to finish essential version 1 features that were allowed to slip without an explicit decision.

Expansion into South Africa should not begin during months four to six unless Zimbabwe execution is materially ahead of plan and a specific funded opportunity exists. The model should be designed for multi-country support, but the organisation should not split focus before local network effects and revenue are visible.


# 27. Governance and decision rights

A platform with advertising, creators, trust decisions and national ambitions needs explicit decision rights. Founder control alone is insufficient once campaigns, complaints and public partnerships scale.

Public-sector partnerships should be documented, time-bound and transparent. Shoppage should avoid dependence on a single ministry, political sponsor, telecom operator, bank or advertising customer. Commercial ranking and moderation rules must remain platform-governed and publicly described.


# 28. Risk register and mitigation

The model is ambitious. The principal risks are operational, not conceptual.


# 29. Decision gates

Management should stop, narrow or change direction when evidence contradicts assumptions. Commitment to the mission does not justify ignoring poor unit economics or buyer trust.


# 30. Final business model

Shoppage is Zimbabwe-owned commerce media infrastructure. Shoppage Central turns merchants into structured commerce nodes. Shoppage Showcase turns products, places and short videos into local buyer discovery. Shoppage Ads captures measurable commercial attention. Shoppage Creators turns local influence and content capability into attributable income. Shoppage Markets digitises dense commercial destinations and communities.

The model stands above a conventional marketplace because it does not depend on native checkout to create value. It stands above classifieds because sellers become persistent, verified operating entities rather than isolated posts. It stands above social media for commerce because content is attached to fresh catalogues and measurable outcomes. It stands above a directory because buyers can search actual products, watch demonstrations, submit demand and contact relevant vendors. It stands above a pure advertising platform because it owns the local commerce graph needed to target purchase intent.

# 56. Strategic substitution objectives

Second, it should significantly reduce the dependence of Zimbabwean merchants on Google Ads and Facebook/Instagram advertising for local customer acquisition. The objective is not to prohibit external platforms or claim that every advertisement can move immediately. It is to offer a superior local performance channel for the use cases where Shoppage has stronger first-party information: current local inventory, exact variants, branch availability, delivery zones, verified sellers, creator attribution, buyer requests, prices, market clusters and transaction or lead outcomes. The implementation package confirms that this advantage cannot come from global reference data alone; it requires first-party and partner offer observations at seller-branch-product-time grain.

## 56.1 The three substitution metrics

### Commercial Discovery Share

The percentage of high-intent local commerce sessions in covered categories and geographies that start on, are resolved by or are referred through Shoppage. This should be measured through search sessions, product-detail views, buyer requests, map interactions, catalogue visits, store contacts and verified outcomes.

### Merchant Advertising Wallet Share

The percentage of a participating merchant's measurable digital acquisition budget allocated to Shoppage. The denominator should include Meta boosts and ads, Google Ads, influencer payments, group-admin promotions, classified listing fees and other paid digital acquisition reported or evidenced by the merchant.

### Local Advertising Value Retention

The percentage of advertiser spend that remains in Zimbabwe after deducting direct imported infrastructure, payment-processing and model-inference costs. Local payroll, creator earnings, merchant services, field-agent commissions, publisher revenue shares and Zimbabwean taxes should count as locally retained value.

## 56.2 Practical substitution milestones

These are management targets, not externally guaranteed forecasts.

- **Months 0-3:** establish a reliable baseline from launch merchants; prove that Shoppage can receive at least 10% of the measurable digital acquisition budget of a cohort of active pilot advertisers; achieve attributable lead or store-action reporting for at least 70% of paid campaigns.

- **Months 4-6:** reach 15-25% advertising-wallet share among retained active advertisers; demonstrate that a meaningful proportion of covered-category product searches in the launch zones resolve without leaving Shoppage; launch keyword, geo, video, creator and buyer-request ad products.

- **Month 12:** target 20-35% advertising-wallet share among high-engagement advertisers; establish Shoppage as one of the first three product-discovery channels named by surveyed buyers in the launch categories and cities; show repeat campaign purchase from at least half of merchants who achieved a verified outcome.

- **Months 24-36:** target category-level leadership in selected cities rather than claiming national dominance prematurely; pursue 25-40% of structured local commercial-discovery activity in mature categories and a similar or higher share of local performance-ad budgets among active Shoppage merchants.

## 56.3 What must remain outside the claim

Shoppage must not publish unsupported estimates of total Google or Meta advertising revenue from Zimbabwe. It must not describe all external ad spend as economic loss, because external platforms provide real value and imported infrastructure will remain part of Shoppage's own cost base. The defensible claim is narrower and stronger: Shoppage will retain more Zimbabwean commercial value by owning local commerce data, merchant tools, creator earnings, campaign measurement and buyer relationships.

# 57. Shoppage product-search and shopping substitution system

The substitute for Google Shopping is not a copied product grid. It is a deeper local commerce-resolution system.

## 57.1 Shoppage commercial search

Commercial search should resolve natural-language and structured requests such as:

- “Who has a 5 kVA inverter in stock near Chitungwiza today?”
- “Compare 195/65 R15 tyres available in Harare with fitting.”
- “Which verified suppliers deliver cement to Ruwa?”
- “Find an original charging port for this exact phone model.”
- “Show furniture makers who can deliver before month end.”
- “Where can I collect this item after work?”

Google can index public pages, but Shoppage should answer using private and operational data that general web search normally does not possess: last stock confirmation, exact variant, service radius, response time, branch-level availability, verified location, merchant reliability, real lead outcomes, compatible alternatives and local delivery constraints.

## 57.2 Search result architecture

Every result should combine:

- Exact and semantic relevance.
- Variant and compatibility matching.
- Distance, delivery zone and collection feasibility.
- Freshness and confidence score.
- Merchant trust and response performance.
- Price validity and currency timestamp.
- Organic quality and clearly labelled sponsored relevance.
- Low-data preview and immediate buyer action.

## 57.3 The Shoppage answer layer

Shoppage Ask should produce a concise commercial answer before presenting listings. It should explain what is available, where, at what confidence level, what alternatives exist and what information remains uncertain. For ambiguous requests, the system should ask practical questions such as budget, area, size, model or delivery date before routing the buyer to sellers.

## 57.4 Product graph depth

The catalogue must distinguish brand, model, generation, size, capacity, colour, condition, specification, compatibility, warranty, branch, seller, currency, price date and stock state. This structured variant depth is central to replacing generic search results and is a major moat because it requires ongoing merchant and field operations rather than one-time web crawling.

## 57.5 Public-web strategy

Shoppage should still publish crawlable, high-quality product, vendor, market and category pages so that external search engines become acquisition channels. The objective is to use Google to acquire a buyer once, then retain the buyer through saved needs, alerts, verified inventory, repeat searches, price tracking and local recommendations.

# 58. Shoppage Ads substitution system

## 58.1 The strategic promise

Shoppage Ads should promise locally measurable commerce outcomes rather than cheap impressions. The primary advertiser proposition is:

> Reach people who are actively looking for products, suppliers, services and markets in Zimbabwe, using current local commerce data and reporting tied to calls, WhatsApp leads, quote requests, visits, reservations and verified sales.

## 58.2 Ad products required to substitute external spend

- Sponsored commercial search with strict relevance thresholds.
- Promoted products and product variants.
- Branch and nearby-availability promotions.
- Category and market sponsorships.
- Short-video commerce campaigns.
- Creator affiliate and managed content campaigns.
- Buyer-request matching and qualified-lead access.
- Retargeting within Shoppage using consented first-party behaviour.
- Saved-search and price-alert sponsorship with frequency controls.
- Market-day, launch, stock-arrival and event campaigns.
- Diaspora-to-Zimbabwe campaigns.
- Brand and distributor campaigns routed to verified local stockists.
- Offline QR and location campaigns with digital attribution.

## 58.3 Why merchants should shift budget

The platform must be easier than Meta Ads Manager for a small seller and more locally informative than general search advertising. A merchant should be able to select a product, choose an objective and area, set a budget, receive a quality warning, publish and see attributable results without understanding campaign jargon.

## 58.4 Outcome measurement

Campaign reporting should distinguish:

- Impression and view.
- Engaged product view.
- Catalogue visit.
- Save or share.
- Phone, map or WhatsApp action.
- Qualified buyer request.
- Vendor response.
- Reservation or quote.
- Verified store visit or completed sale where available.
- Repeat customer or repeat enquiry.

## 58.5 Local ad ecosystem

Shoppage should progressively share value with Zimbabwean creators, field agents, market operators, media publishers and specialist communities. This creates a domestic performance-media network rather than a closed platform that merely redirects foreign-platform economics to one local company.

# 59. International benchmark lessons

The model is feasible because several markets have produced strong local or regional platforms by combining search, commerce, advertising, payments, logistics, content and merchant services around local behaviour.

- South Korea's Naver demonstrates the strategic value of connecting search, shopping, maps, content, advertising and payments around local language and local data.

- Latin America's Mercado Libre demonstrates how a regional commerce platform can combine marketplace demand, merchant tools, advertising, payments, credit and fulfilment into a compounding ecosystem.

- Poland's Allegro demonstrates that a locally entrenched marketplace can remain the leading commerce destination while expanding advertising, loyalty, logistics and AI-assisted shopping capabilities despite global competition.

The lesson is not to copy their mature scope. The lesson is to build a narrow local advantage, gather proprietary first-party data, compound merchant and buyer utility, and expand only after the core loop works. Zimbabwe's smaller market makes focus and capital discipline more important, not less.

# 64. Implementation evidence status as of 10 July 2026

Shoppage now has a runnable commerce-intelligence foundation package. This changes the model from a strategy that proposes a data moat into a strategy with an initial, validated evidence and resolution layer. The package includes source inventories, classification references, repeatable ingestion scripts, validation scripts, a PostgreSQL commerce-graph schema, a million-record global product reference database, organisation and place studies, review queues and a merchant-offer feed contract.

The package was independently re-run against its included validators before this edition was prepared. The global-product, organisation-study, place-hierarchy and package-integrity checks passed. The correct management conclusion is therefore: **the technical foundation is proven at seed and scale-test level, while Zimbabwe commercial truth still depends on merchant, regulator, municipal, distributor, manufacturer, POS and field evidence.**

|---|---|---|

## 64.1 Claims ladder

Every public, investor, government and merchant claim must use one of four states.

- **Foundation validated:** a schema, pipeline, count or test was reproduced from the package.
- **Discovered candidate:** a source indicates that an entity, place, product or relationship may exist.
- **Verified claim:** the specific predicate has sufficient current evidence and a defined verification scope.
- **Production truth:** a verified claim is fresh enough and operationally safe to use in search, advertising, intelligence or fulfilment.

A record must never move from candidate to verified merely because it appeared in a directory, map, Facebook page, website or model output. Verification is predicate-specific. A regulator may verify a licence; a merchant may verify a current catalogue; a field observation may verify a location; a buyer outcome may verify responsiveness. These sources may disagree, and the disagreement must remain visible until resolved.

## 64.2 What this changes in the investment thesis

The defensibility thesis is no longer limited to future user growth. Shoppage has the beginning of an evidence graph with source identity, licence, timestamps, hashes, confidence, review states and unresolved conflicts. Competitors can copy a storefront layout, but copying a governed history of what was observed, by whom, under what rights, at which branch, in which market, at what time, with what confidence and how the claim changed is materially harder.

However, the model must avoid a common investor mistake: counting reference rows as commercial coverage. The value of the graph will be determined by the percentage of Zimbabwean buyer needs it can resolve with fresh local offers, verified suppliers and attributable outcomes, not by the gross number of global reference records.

# 65. Commerce graph architecture locked by implementation

The package validates the model's central architectural decision: Shoppage must be an evidence-backed commerce graph, not a flat listings database.

## 65.1 Stable entities, changing claims

Stable nodes include organisations, branches, places, markets, master products, variants, creators, categories and commercial locations. Time-varying claims include:

- a branch is operating at a location;
- a merchant sells a product or provides a service;
- a variant is in stock;
- a price is valid in a currency;
- a store belongs to a market;
- a creator is authorised to promote an offer;
- a merchant responds within a measured time;
- a buyer outcome occurred;
- a licence or verification remains valid.

This separation is essential. Product identity should not be overwritten when a seller changes price. A business should not be duplicated for every branch. A market should not be declared inside another market because two coordinates are close. A product family should not be treated as a compatible substitute without category evidence.

## 65.2 Required source and evidence controls

Production records must preserve:

- source system and source type;
- source authority and predicate scope;
- external source identifier and source URL where permitted;
- observation and retrieval time;
- content hash or equivalent immutable evidence reference;
- rights and reuse note;
- confidence and verification state;
- reviewer and review decision;
- validity period and supersession history;
- unresolved conflicts.

The source-precedence rule is predicate-specific. There is no universal best source. CIPZ may control legal registration evidence; a regulator controls a licence within its mandate; a merchant controls its current catalogue; a market operator controls a roster; field verification controls what was observed at a time; and transaction evidence may control whether a lead or sale occurred.

## 65.3 Review queues are a product capability

Review queues must be first-class operational products, not hidden database leftovers. Required queues include:

- organisation duplicate and branch-resolution candidates;
- unresolved activity classification;
- product identity and variant conflicts;
- product-enrichment gaps;
- place-parent and market-containment review;
- offer records with invalid, absent or unmatched identifiers;
- stale stock and price claims;
- disputed merchant, creator or customer claims;
- rights and licensing exceptions.

Each queue needs severity, owner, service-level target, evidence display, decision reason and audit history. AI may propose a decision, but high-impact merges, rejections, suspensions and disputed claims require human approval and appeal.

## 65.4 Coverage cells become the national operating map

The implemented schema includes a place-category coverage concept. Shoppage should operationalise this as a coverage cell for every relevant combination of location and commercial category. A coverage cell should track:

- verified organisations;
- active branches and markets;
- fresh offers and variant depth;
- average price and stock recency;
- merchant response rate;
- buyer demand and unresolved requests;
- verified outcomes;
- trust and complaint signals;
- coverage state: empty, thin, usable, strong or authoritative.

Coverage cells guide field operations, merchant acquisition, advertising inventory, creator assignments, city expansion and national reporting. They prevent Shoppage from claiming nationwide coverage when only a few categories or neighbourhoods are useful.

# 66. Organisation intelligence programme

## 66.1 Current baseline

The current study contains 6,101 source records from OpenStreetMap, the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe microfinance register, Zimbabwe Stock Exchange anchors and ZERA petroleum licences. It demonstrates source ingestion, normalisation, provenance and activity classification. It does not yet resolve all records into legal entities, branch structures or active merchant accounts.

The source mix is weighted toward OpenStreetMap discovery. That is useful for coverage, but insufficient for national business truth. The next wave must diversify toward official identity, first-party merchant evidence and active commercial relationships.

## 66.2 Required organisation lifecycle

Every organisation record should move through a controlled lifecycle:

1. **Discovered:** source-backed candidate exists.
2. **Resolved:** duplicate, branch and legal-entity relationships have been reviewed.
3. **Claimed:** an authorised representative controls the Shoppage profile.
4. **Verified:** defined identity, location or licence checks pass.
5. **Activated:** catalogue, contact path and freshness obligations are live.
6. **Performing:** the merchant responds, keeps information current and generates outcomes.
7. **Monitored:** verification, activity and complaint states are continuously reviewed.
8. **Suspended, dormant or closed:** lifecycle change is retained rather than deleted.

## 66.3 Priority source expansion

The organisation programme should add, subject to access and reuse rights:

- CIPZ or Ministry of Justice legal-identity evidence;
- VFEX and additional exchange/company anchors;
- RBZ banks, payment providers and other regulated financial institutions;
- POTRAZ telecom, postal and courier operators;
- ZimTrade exporter and manufacturer partnerships;
- PRAZ and procurement-supplier evidence;
- tourism, medicine, insurance and other sector regulators;
- mall, market, association and distributor rosters;
- merchant self-registration and authorised feeds;
- field-agent and market-captain observations;
- public social and directory discovery followed by merchant confirmation.

## 66.4 Organisation intelligence outputs

Shoppage should be able to answer more than “does this business exist?” It should progressively know:

- legal and trading names, aliases and brands;
- parent, subsidiary, franchise, branch and operator relationships;
- activities mapped to ISIC and Shoppage commercial roles;
- categories, products, services and brands carried;
- operating locations, service areas and delivery zones;
- payment, collection and fulfilment options;
- response, freshness and outcome performance;
- trust, complaint and verification history;
- digital maturity and recommended improvements.

This supports merchant search, B2B sourcing, brand-to-stockist advertising, market intelligence and the Business Improvement Copilot.

# 67. Product, variant and compatibility intelligence

## 67.1 What the million-product seed proves

The million-record database proves that Shoppage can ingest, uniquely identify, index and validate a large global reference source. It includes 950,334 valid GTINs and exposes 883,674 records with one or more enrichment gaps rather than silently filling missing values.

The seed is intentionally global and food-focused. It should be treated as:

- a product-mastering scale test;
- a source of food identifiers and attributes where rights permit;
- a future grocery and packaged-goods reference asset;
- a test corpus for matching, enrichment and search infrastructure.

It should not be treated as the launch catalogue for Zimbabwe, and it does not replace category-specific mastering for Home, Power and Building.

## 67.2 Launch-category correction

The launch wedge remains solar, batteries, inverters, electrical, plumbing, hardware, building materials, appliances, furniture and related services. The product programme must therefore prioritise non-food reference and supplier data immediately.

For each launch category, Shoppage needs:

- brand and manufacturer identity;
- model and manufacturer-part-number structure;
- capacity, voltage, size, dimensions, material and grade;
- pack, unit and minimum-order conventions;
- condition and warranty;
- compatibility relationships;
- safety and certification fields where applicable;
- substitution rules and “not equivalent” constraints;
- local naming, spelling and informal aliases;
- category-specific freshness windows.

Sources should be merchant, distributor and manufacturer feeds, authorised catalogues, standards data under suitable licences and category-expert review. Public listings may help discovery, but descriptions and images must not be copied without rights.

## 67.3 Product-layer discipline

Shoppage must preserve four distinct layers:

- **Product family:** broad navigational need, such as hybrid solar inverter.
- **Master product or model:** a defined manufacturer and model identity.
- **Variant:** commercially meaningful configuration, such as capacity, voltage, colour, pack, size, grade or condition.
- **Offer:** seller, branch, market, price, currency, stock, lead time, terms and observation time.

Family keys support navigation and candidate generation. They do not prove interchangeability. Compatibility and substitution require category-specific rules, exact identifiers and expert review.

## 67.4 Enrichment programme

The enrichment queue should be prioritised by commercial impact, not by random record completion. Priority should reflect:

- active Zimbabwe demand;
- launch-category relevance;
- merchant and brand partnerships;
- search failures and buyer requests;
- advertising demand;
- safety and compatibility risk;
- number of local offers affected;
- expected conversion improvement.

A record with a missing optional marketing field should rank below a widely sold inverter with an ambiguous voltage or compatibility attribute.

# 68. Place, market and geolocal intelligence

The package materialises every 25,019-feature Zimbabwe GeoNames record and creates a 25,210-node, 25,209-edge scaffold with 2,101 settlement nodes and 118 explicitly tagged OpenStreetMap markets or malls. It deliberately creates no unsupported market-in-market containment edges.

Only a small portion of market-parent proposals are based on exact source-city naming; most are proximity candidates requiring review. This is correct behaviour. Proximity is a discovery signal, not proof of containment or operating status.

Municipal, market-operator and mall evidence should control:

- building, section, shop and stall membership;
- market-in-market relationships;
- access points and operating hours;
- pickup and collection points;
- parking, route and delivery constraints;
- temporary market days and seasonal events.

## 68.3 Geolocal model enhancements

Administrative geography must be complemented by commercial geography:

- commercial corridors and supplier streets;
- CBD blocks and industrial clusters;
- growth points and rural service centres;
- informal landmarks and route-based access;
- delivery zones and service areas;
- verified collection points;
- market sections, buildings and stall ranges.

Delivery zones and service areas are overlays, not administrative children. They need effective dates because merchants and logistics partners can change coverage.

# 69. Zimbabwe offer acquisition and Stock Truth

## 69.1 The commercial bottleneck

The global master and discovery graph are valuable, but the decisive dependency is local offer supply. Zimbabwe price, stock, seller, branch and availability data cannot be inferred from a global catalogue. The absence of meaningful public Zimbabwe price coverage in open global datasets reinforces the need for direct partnerships and field operations.

## 69.2 Canonical observation grain

The merchant feed contract correctly defines one observation as:

> one seller-branch product offer observed at one timestamp.

Every production offer observation should retain:

- source offer identifier;
- seller and branch identifiers;
- market or commercial-location identifier where relevant;
- observation timestamp with timezone;
- evidence URL or evidence object reference;
- product name and seller SKU;
- GTIN, MPN or other identifier when available;
- currency and non-negative price;
- stock state;
- quantity, category and structured attributes;
- rights state for description and image reuse;
- resolution state and method.

Supported stock states should include in stock, limited, out of stock, preorder, backorder and unknown. Currency must reflect what the merchant actually displayed; Shoppage should not silently convert or combine currencies without a timestamped rate and a clear display rule.

## 69.3 Acquisition order

Local offer acquisition should follow this order:

1. Merchant-authorised CSV, JSON, API, SFTP or object-storage feeds.
2. Distributor and manufacturer agreements, including identifiers and branch availability.
3. POS and e-commerce connectors installed with merchant authorisation.
4. Merchant mobile updates, WhatsApp-assisted ingestion and structured catalogue entry.
5. Field, receipt and price-tag observations under explicit contributor terms.
6. Public pages only where terms, robots controls, law and intended reuse allow collection.

The model must reject a “scrape first, ask later” strategy. It creates legal, data-quality and investor-diligence risk and does not establish current stock.

## 69.4 Resolution and idempotency

The validated ingester proves three important operating rules:

- valid GTIN exact matches can be resolved deterministically;
- valid identifiers not yet in the master remain visible for review;
- unbarcoded products enter text-resolution review rather than being silently merged;
- repeated ingestion of the same seller-branch-offer-time observation is idempotent.

Production matching should proceed from exact identifiers to supplier/manufacturer identifiers and then to reviewed brand, model, quantity, pack and category evidence. Text similarity alone must not merge high-risk technical products.

## 69.5 Scale interpretation

One million offer observations is an operational volume, not necessarily one million unique products. One hundred participating branches with 10,000 active SKUs represent one million branch-SKU observations in a single refresh. Repeated snapshots create a large price and availability history while product masters remain deduplicated.

The business model should therefore report separately:

- unique master products;
- variants;
- active local offers;
- branches with feeds;
- fresh offer observations;
- historical observations;
- unresolved offers;
- verified outcome events.

# 71. AI, matching and human-review operating model

AI should accelerate evidence processing without becoming a source of unverified truth.

# Part V - Transaction, Fulfilment and Merchant-Site Decisions

# 81. Executive decision: Shoppage controls the workflow, not regulated money or delivery assets

The transaction and fulfilment boundary is now locked.

> **Shoppage must own the buyer journey, order or request context, merchant terms, attribution, trust record and outcome evidence. It must not initially own customer funds, warehouses, delivery vehicles or national settlement risk.**

This is not an off-platform model in the sense of losing the customer, data or commercial outcome. It is an **on-platform orchestration model with partner-executed regulated and asset-heavy services**.

The distinction is critical:

- Shoppage creates and preserves the commercial record.
- The merchant remains the seller and merchant of record unless a later, explicitly contracted model states otherwise.
- A licensed payment provider moves money and returns status to Shoppage.
- The merchant, buyer or approved fulfilment partner performs collection or delivery.
- Shoppage records milestones, proof, complaints, response and partner performance.
- Shoppage does not store card credentials, wallet PINs or pooled customer balances.
- Shoppage does not promise universal delivery where it does not control capacity.

This structure gives Shoppage practical control over conversion and intelligence without exposing the first six months to avoidable banking, custody, vehicle, warehousing, labour, fuel and loss risks.

## 81.1 Locked operating boundary

| Function | Shoppage owns | External party executes |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery and intent | Search, video, product page, buyer request, quote, reservation and attribution | None |
| Commercial record | Basket or request, seller, branch, items, terms, price validity, fulfilment choice and status history | Merchant confirms commercial terms |
| Payment experience | Method presentation, payment initiation, reference, webhook status, receipt link and dispute case | Licensed provider processes and settles funds |
| Fulfilment experience | Options, quote request, partner selection, dispatch status, tracking, proof and service score | Merchant, buyer, courier, installer or collection point performs physical movement |
| Trust and redress | Identity, warnings, evidence, complaints, response deadlines, outcome history and ranking effects | Merchant, provider and partner supply evidence and remedies within their legal scope |
| Financial liability | Platform fees, advertising and contracted service obligations | Seller bears product-sale obligations; provider bears regulated payment obligations; carrier bears contracted delivery obligations |

## 81.2 Why not fully off-platform

A simple WhatsApp handoff followed by invisible payment and delivery would preserve neither the outcome moat nor customer protection. Shoppage would know that a click occurred but not whether the merchant responded, the price changed, payment succeeded, the parcel arrived or the buyer was satisfied.

Therefore, Shoppage must maintain a lightweight but complete transaction envelope even when execution happens elsewhere. Every meaningful conversion should create a persistent record with:

- buyer or anonymous session reference;
- merchant and branch;
- product, variant or service;
- quoted price, currency and validity;
- quantity and relevant specifications;
- selected payment method;
- selected fulfilment method;
- status timeline;
- evidence and communication references;
- complaint or cancellation state;
- final outcome and attribution.

The transaction envelope is a core intelligence asset. It connects discovery, advertising, creator activity, merchant behaviour, payment choice, delivery performance and buyer outcomes.

# 82. Payment architecture decision

## 82.1 Final decision

Payments should be **visible and initiated inside Shoppage but processed and settled by licensed external providers** for at least the 0-6 month implementation period.

Shoppage should not launch its own wallet, hold customer money, pool merchant settlements or become an unlicensed payment intermediary. The platform should integrate several rails through provider adapters and hosted or tokenised flows.

Zimbabwe already has local online-payment infrastructure that can support partner processing. Zimswitch publicly offers Zimswitch Online and recurring-payment services, and describes itself as the national electronic funds switch and clearing house designated by the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe. Paynow publicly offers merchant services and e-commerce solutions. These should be treated as procurement and integration candidates, not as permanent exclusive dependencies.

## 82.2 Payment methods to support

| Method | 0-3 month treatment | 4-6 month treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Cash at collection | Merchant declares acceptance; buyer receives collection terms; merchant confirms outcome | Collection code and merchant confirmation with fraud controls |
| Cash on delivery | Only where merchant or delivery partner accepts responsibility | Partner status, proof and failed-delivery reason captured |
| Mobile money | Payment instructions or hosted provider flow; no PIN collection by Shoppage | Provider API or hosted checkout with signed status webhook |
| Bank / ZIPIT transfer | Merchant bank details or provider-generated reference; buyer can submit proof | Automated reference matching where partner APIs permit |
| Card | Hosted checkout or provider page; no raw card data enters Shoppage | Tokenised or hosted single-vendor checkout with provider status |
| Pay-by-link | Merchant or Shoppage-generated provider link attached to order or quote | Unified status and reconciliation dashboard |
| Deposit | Merchant-defined deposit amount and terms; clear risk warning | Integrated deposit flow for approved categories and merchants |
| Protected payment / escrow | Not offered as a Shoppage-held service | Limited pilot only through a regulated partner with explicit terms |

## 82.3 Merchant-of-record rule

For normal marketplace and lead flows, the vendor remains the merchant of record. The vendor controls product legality, pricing, tax treatment, warranties, refunds, stock and fulfilment promises. Shoppage supplies the platform, advertising, workflow and evidence layer.

This rule must be visible in contracts, checkout copy, receipts and dispute procedures. Shoppage should not accidentally become the seller merely because its interface initiated a payment.

Exceptions should require a separate approved business model, such as a future Shoppage-managed procurement service or a contracted diaspora concierge. Those exceptions need distinct terms, accounting, insurance and consumer-protection controls.

## 82.4 Checkout design

The practical sequence is:

1. The buyer selects a product, quote or reservation.
2. Shoppage verifies the current merchant, branch, price-validity and fulfilment state.
3. The buyer selects payment and fulfilment methods.
4. Shoppage creates a single-vendor order or transaction intent.
5. The payment provider receives only the fields necessary to process payment.
6. The provider returns a signed success, pending, failed or expired status.
7. Shoppage updates the transaction envelope and notifies merchant and buyer.
8. Fulfilment begins only under the merchant or partner's approved rules.
9. Outcome, refund, cancellation or complaint remains linked to the original record.

## 82.5 Multi-vendor basket rule

Shoppage may allow a customer to save products from several merchants in one planning basket, but the first transaction implementation should split checkout into separate merchant orders. This avoids pooled settlement, split refunds, cross-merchant delivery promises and unclear merchant-of-record responsibility.

A universal multi-vendor cart should not be built before Shoppage has:

- reliable stock reservation;
- provider-supported split settlement;
- merchant payout and reconciliation controls;
- returns and partial-refund capability;
- fraud and chargeback operations;
- clear tax and contractual treatment;
- enough transaction volume to justify the complexity.

## 82.6 Payment data and security

Shoppage should store:

- payment provider;
- provider transaction reference;
- method category;
- amount and currency;
- status and timestamp;
- signed callback verification result;
- refund or reversal status;
- merchant reconciliation state;
- evidence and dispute links.

Shoppage should not store:

- full card numbers;
- CVV values;
- mobile-money PINs;
- online-banking passwords;
- provider secrets in client applications;
- unencrypted payment evidence containing unnecessary personal data.

## 82.7 Payment revenue

Payment should not be treated as the primary early revenue engine. Subscriptions, ads, onboarding, managed campaigns and qualified demand remain stronger 0-3 month revenue products.

Later revenue may include:

- disclosed convenience or orchestration fee;
- contracted payment-provider referral share;
- transaction commission where Shoppage materially controls conversion;
- protected-payment fee through a regulated partner;
- reconciliation and payment-automation subscription for merchants.

Fees must remain transparent and must not make the local platform more expensive than the value it creates.

# 83. Fulfilment and last-mile decision

## 83.1 Final decision

Fulfilment should be **partner-led and merchant-led, but orchestrated and measured by Shoppage**. Shoppage should not buy a fleet, operate a warehouse or promise national same-day delivery during the first six months.

The platform should establish a **Shoppage Fulfilment Network**. This is a software, standards and partner network - not an owned courier company.

## 83.2 Supported fulfilment modes

| Fulfilment mode | Best use | Shoppage role |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer collection | High-trust local purchases, bulky items and cash transactions | Verify branch, hours, reservation, collection code and outcome |
| Merchant delivery | Furniture, appliances, building materials and vendors with own vehicles | Capture zone, charge, lead time, status and proof |
| Courier partner | Small parcels and predictable urban routes | Quote, assign, track, receive proof and score service |
| Market collection point | Dense markets, informal addresses and consolidated routes | Verify point, custody handoff, hours and collection confirmation |
| Installer fulfilment | Solar, electrical, plumbing and home-improvement services | Schedule assessment, installation, proof and workmanship feedback |
| Long-distance transport partner | Intercity parcels and diaspora-supported purchases | Record handoff, route, collection point, recipient and proof |
| Customer-arranged transport | Bulk goods or specialised haulage | Preserve quote and terms; label responsibility clearly |

## 83.3 Category-specific fulfilment

Shoppage must not force one parcel model across all categories.

### Home, power and building

- Solar systems often require assessment, installation and commissioning rather than simple parcel delivery.
- Building materials require quantity confirmation, loading, vehicle type, distance, access and offloading terms.
- Furniture and appliances need dimensions, handling, floor access, assembly and damage evidence.
- Electrical and plumbing items may be collected, couriered or bundled with an installer.

The product model must therefore distinguish delivery, installation, collection, haulage and service fulfilment.

### Small parcels and electronics

Small items can use standard partner rates, scheduled pickup, tracking and proof. Shoppage can automate this category earlier because dimensions, weight and route are more predictable.

### Informal markets

Market collection points and market captains can reduce failed navigation and create trusted handoff locations. A stall is not automatically a secure collection point; the operator, custody process and opening hours must be verified.

## 83.4 0-3 month fulfilment scope

Build:

- merchant-declared fulfilment methods;
- collection, delivery and installation flags;
- delivery zones and indicative charges;
- request-a-delivery-quote workflow;
- partner directory and service-area records;
- reservation and collection reference;
- manual status updates;
- proof upload for collection, delivery or installation;
- complaint and failed-fulfilment reasons;
- partner and merchant performance metrics.

Do not build:

- route optimisation for an owned fleet;
- warehouse management;
- national dispatch control;
- universal real-time pricing;
- Shoppage-employed drivers;
- unconditional delivery guarantees.

## 83.5 4-6 month fulfilment scope

Pilot in Harare:

- courier and merchant-delivery adapters;
- structured rate cards and quote comparison;
- pickup scheduling;
- dispatch and status webhooks;
- proof of delivery or collection;
- failed-attempt reason codes;
- recipient confirmation;
- selected verified pickup points;
- partner SLA scorecards;
- claims workflow;
- category-specific delivery and installation templates.

## 83.6 When owned capacity may be considered

Owned vehicles or warehousing should remain an exception. Before approval, management must show:

- sustained route density in a defined zone;
- partner failure or pricing that materially harms conversion;
- positive contribution after vehicles, drivers, fuel, maintenance, insurance, losses, supervision and idle time;
- category-specific demand that cannot be served through contracted capacity;
- regulatory and insurance readiness;
- a separate P&L and stop condition.

Even then, contracted dedicated routes or franchise capacity should be tested before fleet ownership. Shoppage's main competitive advantage is commerce intelligence and orchestration, not vehicle ownership.

## 83.7 Fulfilment intelligence moat

Every fulfilment event should improve the graph:

- actual delivery coverage by merchant and partner;
- real lead times by route and category;
- failed-delivery and address-friction patterns;
- collection-point reliability;
- damage and complaint rates;
- installation availability and quality;
- cost-to-serve by area;
- preferred fulfilment method by customer segment;
- demand corridors and underserved areas.

This intelligence will eventually allow Shoppage to recommend the best fulfilment method rather than merely listing options.

# 84. Merchant website strategy

## 84.1 Final decision

Every merchant should receive a **Shoppage Merchant Site** as part of Shoppage Central. For most small and medium merchants, this should be sufficient to act as their official reference website.

The merchant site is not a disposable marketplace profile. It should be a high-quality, indexable, shareable, domain-capable commercial website connected to the live Shoppage graph.

The default structure should support:

- `shoppage.co.zw/merchant-name` or an equivalent stable path;
- optional `merchant-name.shoppage.co.zw` subdomain;
- custom domain connection on paid plans;
- organisation and trust profile;
- branches, maps, hours and contact methods;
- products, families, variants and current offers;
- services and capability pages;
- short videos and collections;
- delivery, collection and payment methods;
- policies, warranties and FAQs;
- RFQ, reservation, lead and checkout actions;
- structured data for search engines;
- low-data and accessible rendering;
- merchant analytics and export.

## 84.2 Shoppage as the merchant's reference website

The Merchant Site should be the canonical commercial source for merchants that do not have their own website. It gives the market one current reference for:

- what the merchant is;
- where it operates;
- what it sells;
- which variants are available;
- what prices or price rules apply;
- how buyers can pay, collect or request delivery;
- what evidence and trust state applies;
- how the merchant should be contacted.

This strengthens Shoppage's information moat because merchant updates feed the national graph directly instead of remaining trapped in isolated websites or social posts.

## 84.3 Merchants that already have websites

Shoppage should not force capable merchants to abandon existing websites. Instead, it should support three modes:

| Merchant situation | Recommended mode | Data rule |
|---|---|---|
| No website | Shoppage Merchant Site is the official reference site | Shoppage Central is the source of truth |
| Weak or outdated website | Replace or redirect to a Shoppage Merchant Site with custom domain | Catalogue and merchant data remain in Shoppage |
| Strong existing website | Connect by feed, API, import or embed; keep Shoppage as verified commerce node | One agreed system controls each field to avoid conflicting truth |

Useful integrations include:

- product and offer feeds;
- “available on Shoppage” badges;
- stock or quote widgets;
- verified-location and review widgets;
- creator and video embeds;
- Shoppage Ads conversion events;
- merchant-site links back to the full Shoppage marketplace.

## 84.4 Site ownership and portability

Merchants should retain rights to their original brand assets and first-party business content. Contracts should grant Shoppage the rights needed to display, distribute and improve that content while the service is active.

To build trust and reduce lock-in objections, paid merchants should be able to export appropriate catalogue, lead and content data subject to privacy, third-party rights and platform-security restrictions. Portability does not require exposing Shoppage's derived intelligence, fraud models, rankings or network-wide graph.

## 84.5 Why Merchant Sites are strategically important

Merchant Sites:

- create immediate standalone merchant value before marketplace traffic is large;
- replace low-quality or absent websites;
- improve search-engine acquisition while reducing long-term dependence on Google and Facebook;
- give every QR code, video and advertisement a stable destination;
- keep catalogue, trust, payment and fulfilment data synchronized;
- create recurring subscription value;
- generate first-party behaviour and conversion evidence;
- make Shoppage useful to formal and informal merchants without separate software procurement.

# 85. Pemosites decision

## 85.1 Final decision

Use **both** Shoppage Merchant Sites and Pemosites, but do not build them as competing systems and do not create a separate operating company during the 0-3 month launch.

The recommended structure is:

- **Shoppage Merchant Sites:** native, standardized commercial sites included in Shoppage Central.
- **Pemosites:** an optional premium website-creation and managed-web product, powered by the Shoppage data and identity layer.

Pemosites may use a separate brand and later a separate P&L. It should initially remain a product line, division or wholly owned brand rather than an independent company with duplicated technology, sales, support and data.

## 85.2 Pemosites purpose

Pemosites is for organisations that need more than a marketplace-linked merchant site, including:

- corporate and institutional websites;
- richer service-business websites;
- custom brand presentation;
- editorial pages, news and resources;
- careers and application forms;
- bookings and enquiries;
- multiple languages;
- custom domain and email setup;
- managed content, photography and SEO;
- integrations beyond Shoppage commerce.

It can serve businesses outside Shoppage's initial retail categories while still connecting eligible commerce data back to Shoppage.

## 85.3 One data core, two experiences

Pemosites must not create a second merchant, product or offer database. The architecture should be:

- Shoppage controls organisation, branch, product, offer, trust, payment-method and fulfilment records.
- Pemosites controls page layout, editorial content, theme, navigation, forms and premium web services.
- Shared identities and permissions prevent duplicate logins and contradictory records.
- Pemosites consumes Shoppage APIs and events.
- Updates to a product, branch or offer propagate to both the Shoppage marketplace and the merchant's Pemosites website.
- Pemosites-specific pages do not automatically become national commerce claims unless they pass Shoppage evidence rules.

## 85.4 Commercial model for Pemosites

Potential products:

| Package | Customer | Revenue model |
|---|---|---|
| Launch site | Small business needing a credible web presence | Setup fee plus monthly hosting/support |
| Commerce site | Merchant needing custom branding around Shoppage catalogue | Subscription plus theme, domain and integration fee |
| Managed growth site | Business needing content, SEO and campaigns | Monthly retainer with defined deliverables |
| Corporate site | Larger organisation needing custom structure and governance | Project fee plus support agreement |
| Market or association site | Commercial cluster needing public site plus Shoppage market data | Setup, subscription and sponsorship tools |

## 85.5 0-3 month Pemosites scope

Do not launch a general Wix or WordPress competitor. Build the shared site engine required for Shoppage Merchant Sites:

- responsive templates;
- page and section blocks;
- merchant profile pages;
- product and service pages;
- custom domain mapping;
- navigation and policy pages;
- lead and form components;
- analytics;
- SEO and structured data;
- safe media management;
- theme variables;
- preview and publish workflow.

This produces the technical foundation for Pemosites without distracting the launch.

## 85.6 4-6 month Pemosites pilot

Pilot Pemosites with 10-20 businesses that have clear requirements beyond the standard Merchant Site. Measure:

- setup time;
- monthly support hours;
- gross margin;
- retention;
- content-update frequency;
- lead and search performance;
- demand for custom design;
- overlap with Shoppage plans;
- engineering distraction;
- willingness to pay.

A separate company or full spinout should be considered only when Pemosites has:

- at least 100 paying sites or equivalent contracted annual revenue;
- a repeatable acquisition channel;
- standardized delivery rather than bespoke agency work;
- sustainable gross margin;
- a distinct customer and product strategy;
- clean data, IP, support and cross-selling agreements with Shoppage.

## 85.7 Brand and governance rule

The market must understand that:

- Shoppage is the national commerce network and intelligence platform.
- A Shoppage Merchant Site is the standard commercial website inside that network.
- Pemosites is the optional premium website-building service.

Pemosites must not dilute Shoppage's marketplace brand or create uncertainty about where catalogue truth lives.

# Part VI - Shoppage Platform Framework and Ecosystem Architecture

# 87. Executive decision: Shoppage becomes a commerce operating framework

The strategic direction is now expanded beyond a single marketplace or a fixed portfolio of five products.

> **Shoppage will be built as a reusable national commerce operating framework on which Shoppage-owned products, subsidiaries, independent companies, market operators, creators, software providers and institutional partners can build specialised business models.**

The public product family remains:

- Shoppage Central;
- Shoppage Showcase;
- Shoppage Ads;
- Shoppage Creators; and
- Shoppage Markets.

Under those public layers sits a shared infrastructure layer called the **Shoppage Framework**, also described operationally as the **Shoppage Commerce OS**. The framework contains the identity, organisation, place, product, offer, trust, search, advertising, transaction, fulfilment, intelligence, AI and audit capabilities required by the public products and by approved extensions.

The purpose is not to turn Shoppage into an unfocused conglomerate. The purpose is to ensure that a new commerce venture does not repeatedly rebuild merchant identity, product data, locations, payments, delivery workflows, search, verification, analytics and AI from zero.

A specialised business should be able to launch by composing existing Shoppage capabilities and adding only its differentiated domain logic.

Examples include:

- a construction-procurement network using Shoppage organisations, products, requests, quotes, trust and fulfilment;
- a premium website business such as Pemosites using Shoppage merchant identity, catalogue, media, branches, leads and analytics;
- a market-operator application using Shoppage places, stalls, merchants, events, ads and visitor intelligence;
- a distributor ordering portal using Shoppage products, variants, branches, stock, pricing and merchant accounts;
- a creator-led vertical storefront using Shoppage media, affiliate attribution, product tagging and payouts;
- a logistics partner application using Shoppage delivery requests, routes, proof and service scores;
- a diaspora procurement service using Shoppage verification, quotes, payment-provider integrations and fulfilment evidence.

## 87.1 The central platform principle

The framework should make it easy to create new value while making it difficult to fragment the core.

Every approved product or business built on Shoppage must reuse common records wherever they exist:

- one organisation identity;
- one branch and place graph;
- one canonical product and variant graph;
- one merchant-offer and availability model;
- one trust and reputation history;
- one event and attribution language;
- one permission system;
- one evidence and provenance model;
- one payment-orchestration boundary;
- one fulfilment-orchestration boundary;
- one analytics and AI-governance standard.

A subsidiary may own a customer experience, operating team, brand, revenue stream and domain-specific workflows. It must not silently create a competing merchant database, product catalogue, identity system, trust score or transaction record.

## 87.2 Why this strengthens defensibility

Competitors may recreate a storefront, classified listing page, short-video feed or ad dashboard. They will find it materially harder to recreate a platform where every additional company, plugin and subsidiary increases the completeness and usefulness of the same evidence-backed commerce graph.

The compounding loop becomes:

1. Shoppage acquires verified merchants, places, products and demand.
2. A product or partner uses those assets to serve a specialised need.
3. Its operations produce new authorised observations, outcomes and relationships.
4. The shared graph becomes more complete and accurate.
5. Search, recommendations, risk models, advertising and merchant advice improve.
6. The framework becomes more attractive to the next partner or subsidiary.
7. Launch cost and time decline while switching cost and information advantage rise.

This is a stronger moat than a collection of isolated applications.

# 88. Framework architecture

## 88.1 Five architectural planes

The Shoppage Framework should be designed in five planes.

| Plane | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Commerce kernel | Canonical records and rules that must remain consistent | Organisations, branches, places, products, variants, offers, identities, permissions, evidence and audit |
| Shared platform services | Reusable operational capabilities | Search, media, messaging, trust, ads, leads, requests, payments, fulfilment, billing, analytics and AI |
| Extension layer | Stable interfaces through which new capabilities connect | APIs, webhooks, SDKs, event contracts, data connectors, provider adapters and plugin runtime |
| Experience layer | User-facing products and vertical applications | Central, Showcase, Ads, Creators, Markets, Pemosites and approved verticals |
| Ecosystem and governance | Commercial, security and quality rules for participants | Developer portal, certification, revenue share, privacy, support, dispute and termination controls |

## 88.2 Commerce kernel

The kernel is the most protected part of Shoppage. It should be modular in code but centrally governed in semantics.

The kernel includes:

- person and account identity;
- merchant and organisation identity;
- legal entity, trading name, branch and facility relationships;
- country, province, city, suburb, market, building, section, store and stall hierarchy;
- global master product, product family, variant and compatibility relationships;
- merchant-specific offer, price, stock state, service area and validity;
- evidence, source, licence, review and confidence state;
- roles, permissions, consent and data-purpose controls;
- immutable event and change history;
- trust cases, complaints, responses and outcomes;
- canonical currency, unit, category and attribute dictionaries.

No plugin should directly mutate kernel data without passing through validated domain services. The objective is not central bureaucracy; it is data integrity.

## 88.3 Shared platform services

The initial service catalogue should include:

| Service | Reusable capability | Primary users |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and access | Registration, authentication, organisation membership, roles, consent and delegated access | Every Shoppage product and partner |
| Commerce graph | Entity lookup, relationship traversal, evidence, history and resolution | Search, intelligence, verticals and integrations |
| Catalogue and offers | Product matching, merchant listings, variants, price, stock, branch and freshness | Merchants, marketplaces, websites and procurement tools |
| Place intelligence | Geo hierarchy, nearby search, delivery zones, market containment and branch discovery | Central, Markets, fulfilment and partner apps |
| Search and discovery | Keyword, semantic, faceted, geo and recommendation services | Showcase, merchant sites and vertical applications |
| Media and content | Images, short video, documents, moderation, transformations and product tagging | Showcase, Creators, Ads, Pemosites and merchants |
| Trust and safety | Verification, reports, risk signals, review evidence, disputes and enforcement | All transaction and discovery products |
| Leads and requests | Enquiries, RFQs, reservations, buyer needs, routing, response and outcome tracking | Central, verticals, creators and markets |
| Advertising | Campaigns, targeting, inventory, auction or rule-based allocation, attribution and billing | Ads, partner publishers and verticals |
| Creator and affiliate | Creator identity, tagged products, links, attribution, disclosure and earnings | Creators, merchants and subsidiaries |
| Payment orchestration | Method presentation, provider initiation, status, receipt and reconciliation reference | Merchant sites, procurement and transaction products |
| Fulfilment orchestration | Delivery options, quotes, dispatch, tracking, proof and service performance | Merchants, partners and verticals |
| Billing and entitlements | Plans, usage, limits, invoices, revenue share and partner settlement records | Shoppage and ecosystem businesses |
| Analytics and intelligence | Events, funnels, dashboards, benchmarks, demand and merchant advice | Merchants, brands, markets, investors and partners |
| AI platform | Model gateway, prompts, retrieval, grounding, evaluation, cost, privacy and human review | Every AI-assisted workflow |
| Notification and communication | Email, SMS, push, WhatsApp-compatible handoff, templates and preference controls | Buyers, merchants, creators and operations |

## 88.4 Experience composition

A new application should compose platform capabilities rather than fork the platform.

A market-insurance referral product, for example, may need:

- organisation and merchant identity;
- market and branch relationships;
- consent and verification;
- risk-relevant operational evidence;
- lead generation;
- a regulated partner adapter;
- status and attribution;
- privacy-safe analytics.

It should not build a new market directory and identity database solely for insurance referrals.

## 89.5 Plugin lifecycle

5. **Limited pilot:** small merchant or partner cohort with feature flags.
6. **Certification:** published availability with capability and risk labels.
7. **Monitoring:** reliability, complaints, misuse and commercial performance reviewed.
8. **Renewal or removal:** certification renewed, restricted or revoked.

| Strategic | Deeply integrated partner with contractual service levels and joint operating plan | Critical payment, fulfilment, data or national-scale services |

# 90. Subsidiary and venture-studio model

## 90.1 Strategic purpose

The framework allows Shoppage to incubate specialised businesses without weakening the core company.

A subsidiary should exist only where separate management, capital, regulation, liability, partnerships or economics create a real advantage. A new brand is not sufficient justification.

## 90.2 Recommended corporate architecture

Subject to legal, tax and regulatory advice, the long-term group structure should distinguish platform IP from operating ventures.

| Entity | Primary responsibility | What it owns or controls |
|---|---|---|
| Shoppage Holdings | Group governance, capital allocation, trademarks and subsidiary ownership | Group equity and strategic control |
| Shoppage PlatformCo | Core software, commerce graph, APIs, AI platform, developer ecosystem and licensing | Platform IP, shared schemas, core contracts and platform operations |
| Shoppage Zimbabwe OperatingCo | Central, Showcase, Ads, Creators, Markets and local merchant/buyer operations | Zimbabwe operating relationships, sales, support and local execution |
| Approved subsidiaries | Distinct vertical products with their own P&L and accountable leadership | Domain workflows, customer proposition and contracted operating assets |
| External powered partners | Independent companies using APIs or white-label services | Their own business, customers and domain-specific IP subject to platform terms |

This structure should not be implemented prematurely if it creates cost and administrative burden. During months 0-6, the group can operate through one company with internal cost centres, IP registers and clear product accounts. The structure becomes relevant when fundraising, regulation, liability, partner ownership or a proven subsidiary business justifies separation.

## 90.3 Subsidiary admission tests

A proposed subsidiary must pass all of the following tests.

- It serves a distinct customer problem with a coherent proposition.
- It can show a credible route to independent revenue or strategic value.
- At least 60-70% of its non-domain infrastructure can reuse the Shoppage Framework.
- It does not fragment merchant, product, place, trust or outcome data.
- It has a named accountable leader and measurable P&L.
- It has explicit data, IP, brand and platform-licence agreements.
- Its liabilities and regulatory obligations are understood.
- Its existence improves focus or capital access rather than merely increasing organisational complexity.
- It has a stop, merge or reintegration condition.

## 90.4 Venture maturity stages

| Stage | Operating form | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Concept | Product brief inside PlatformCo or OperatingCo | Problem evidence, core reuse map and risk review |
| Incubated module | Feature-flagged internal product | Working workflow and early user value |
| Business line | Dedicated owner and internal P&L | Repeat use, revenue signal and support process |
| Subsidiary candidate | Separate governance and investment case | Distinct economics, leadership and legal rationale |
| Subsidiary | Contracted platform licence and group controls | Board approval, capital plan, audits and service agreements |
| Externalisation or partnership | Licensed, joint venture or strategic partner model | Better economics or capability than internal ownership |

# 91. Ecosystem commercial model

## 91.1 Framework revenue streams

The platform framework adds revenue that is distinct from ordinary marketplace subscriptions and advertising.

| Revenue stream | Customer | Charging basis |
|---|---|---|
| Platform licence | Subsidiary, white-label operator or powered partner | Fixed licence plus usage or revenue component |
| API usage | Software provider, brand, distributor or institution | Included allowance, volume tier or contracted capacity |
| Plugin marketplace share | Plugin developer and merchant | Percentage of subscription or transaction revenue |
| Integration services | Enterprise merchant or partner | Setup, data migration, custom connector and support fees |
| Managed hosting and operations | White-label or institutional deployment | Monthly platform and service fee |
| Data and intelligence products | Brands, distributors, markets and institutions | Subscription, report, dashboard or research engagement |
| Certification and assurance | Integration providers | Cost-recovery review and renewal fee, not pay-to-pass |
| Transaction orchestration | Approved transaction products | Transparent fee or revenue share where permitted |
| Advertising inventory federation | Partner publishers and verticals | Revenue share based on attributable delivery and outcomes |
| AI service usage | High-volume partners or subsidiaries | Usage, seat or capability bundle |

## 91.2 Revenue-share principles

Ecosystem economics must reward contribution without encouraging fraud or low-quality volume.

Revenue shares may consider:

- who acquired the merchant or buyer;
- who produced the content;
- who supplied the verified data;
- who delivered the advertising inventory;
- who generated the qualified action;
- who performed regulated payment or delivery execution;
- who carried support, refund or dispute obligations;
- whether the outcome was validated.

The platform must publish the attribution rule for each programme. Hidden discretionary payouts will undermine creator, developer and partner trust.

## 91.3 Subsidiary transfer pricing

Each subsidiary or internal business line should pay or be allocated a transparent platform charge covering:

- shared cloud and infrastructure;
- identity and security;
- core development;
- data operations;
- AI and media usage;
- trust and safety;
- finance, legal and group services;
- brand and distribution benefits.

This makes subsidiary economics credible to investors and prevents a weak business from appearing profitable by consuming free platform resources.

## 91.4 Merchant and user pricing protection

The ecosystem must not create a stack of overlapping fees that makes Shoppage more expensive than the platforms it seeks to displace.

Rules:

- merchants should see the total cost before enabling a plugin;
- overlapping platform and plugin charges must be disclosed;
- core functions cannot be silently removed and resold through compulsory plugins;
- cancellation and data export should be practical;
- revenue-share arrangements should not bias organic search without disclosure;
- sponsored distribution must remain labelled;
- high switching costs should result from accumulated value and workflow integration, not data hostage practices.

# 92. Data, rights and intelligence-sharing model

## 92.1 One graph, controlled views

The framework should operate one canonical commerce graph but provide different authorised views.

A market operator may see its merchants, spaces, events and aggregate demand. A merchant sees its own catalogue, leads and performance. A brand may receive aggregated category intelligence. A fulfilment partner sees only the delivery data required for accepted jobs.

Shared infrastructure does not imply universal visibility.

## 92.2 Data classes

| Data class | Example | Default control |
|---|---|---|
| Public reference | Published branch address, product specification or market location | Searchable subject to source rights and correction |
| Merchant-controlled | Private stock quantity, margin, customer notes or unpublished price | Merchant-authorised access only |
| Transactional | Quote, payment state, delivery proof or complaint | Purpose-limited parties and retention |
| Platform operational | Logs, fraud signals, reliability and support history | Restricted operational access |
| Aggregated intelligence | Area demand, price bands or category gaps | Privacy-safe thresholds and contractual use |
| Partner-contributed | POS feed, courier status or verification result | Contract, provenance, purpose and onward-use rules |
| Sensitive or regulated | Identity documents, financial evidence or protected personal data | Strong controls, minimal collection and jurisdiction-specific compliance |

## 92.3 Contribution-back rule

A powered application should contribute authorised improvements back to the common graph when those improvements concern shared entities.

Examples:

- a verified branch opening updates the organisation-place relationship;
- a merchant-approved product match improves the offer-master link;
- a successful delivery updates fulfilment performance;
- a corrected product attribute improves the shared master after review;
- a resolved identity conflict improves organisation resolution.

The application may retain its proprietary workflow, models and customer-specific private data. The framework should not appropriate data beyond contract, consent and lawful purpose.

## 92.4 Intelligence flywheel safeguards

The value of the graph must not become an excuse for uncontrolled data collection.

Every new data field or derived model requires:

- a defined commerce purpose;
- source and rights classification;
- retention rule;
- access policy;
- confidence and freshness treatment;
- correction and challenge path;
- privacy review where personal data is involved;
- model monitoring where automated decisions are involved.

# 93. Security, reliability and platform governance

## 93.1 Platform constitution

All first-party products, subsidiaries and plugins must follow a common platform constitution.

1. Canonical entities are reused, not duplicated.
2. Evidence and provenance accompany important factual claims.
3. Sponsored distribution is labelled.
4. Sensitive actions are authorised and audited.
5. Tenant and merchant data are isolated by default.
6. External applications receive the minimum required scopes.
7. Payment and fulfilment execution follow the approved boundary.
8. AI cannot invent identity, stock, price, verification or outcomes.
9. Breaking API changes follow a published version and migration policy.
10. Every extension has an owner, support path, kill switch and removal plan.
11. Critical services have service levels, backups and recovery procedures.
12. Data export and merchant continuity are designed before termination occurs.
13. Political, commercial or partner pressure cannot secretly manipulate trust or organic ranking.
14. Platform access may be suspended for fraud, abuse, security or rights violations under published rules.
15. Group subsidiaries receive no undisclosed ranking advantage over equivalent external participants.

## 93.2 Technical isolation

The preferred architecture remains a modular monolith for the initial build, with strict domain boundaries and an event outbox. Plugin support does not require premature microservices.

Implementation controls:

- all external access through an API gateway or equivalent policy layer;
- organisation and environment-scoped credentials;
- OAuth scopes and short-lived tokens where appropriate;
- signed webhooks and replay protection;
- idempotency keys for writes;
- rate limits and quotas;
- input validation and schema contracts;
- encryption in transit and at rest;
- secret management outside source code;
- audit logs for privileged actions;
- dependency and vulnerability scanning;
- feature flags and rapid revocation;
- background-job isolation and retry controls;
- observability by application, tenant and integration;
- backup and restoration testing.

## 93.3 Partner risk tiers

| Risk tier | Examples | Required controls |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Public catalogue widget or read-only branch lookup | Basic identity, rate limit and terms |
| Moderate | Merchant-authorised catalogue write, CRM or analytics connector | OAuth scopes, audit, data review and support path |
| High | Payment, identity, fulfilment, complaint or sensitive data integration | Contract, security assessment, service levels, incident duties and manual approval |
| Critical | National-scale payment switch, official registry, core logistics or public infrastructure | Executive governance, resilience testing, fallback, audit rights and continuity plan |

## 93.4 Neutrality and ecosystem trust

Shoppage-owned subsidiaries will sometimes compete with external ecosystem companies. That conflict must be governed openly.

Required safeguards:

- published organic-ranking factors at an appropriate level;
- clear sponsored labels;
- separation between confidential partner data and competing product teams;
- access logs and role restrictions;
- no use of private partner data to copy a business outside agreed purposes;
- objective certification criteria;
- appeal and complaint process;
- board-level review for material self-preferencing risks;
- contractual protection of merchant and developer data.

## 94.2 Product-council governance

A monthly Platform Council should decide:

- whether a requested feature belongs in the core, a plugin or a vertical;
- whether a new entity or data field is canonical or application-specific;
- whether an integration creates unacceptable security or rights risk;
- whether a vertical should remain a module, become a business line or separate;
- whether a partner receives certification or expanded scopes;
- whether platform investment is producing reuse and moat growth;
- whether competing internal and external products are treated fairly.

The council should include product, engineering, data, commercial, security, legal/privacy and operations representatives.

## 94.3 Build-versus-partner rule

Shoppage should build a capability internally when it is:

- central to the commerce graph or trust model;
- a strategic differentiator;
- required across several products;
- too risky to delegate without control;
- poorly served by local partners;
- likely to generate important proprietary learning.

Shoppage should integrate or partner when the capability is:

- regulated and already supplied by licensed providers;
- asset-heavy;
- commodity infrastructure;
- faster and safer to procure;
- outside the company's distinctive information advantage;
- better operated by specialists under measurable contracts.

# 95. Implementation roadmap: 0-6 months

## 95.1 Months 0-3: fully functional internal framework

The first three months should produce a functioning platform framework for all first-party Shoppage layers and at least one controlled partner or subsidiary-style application.

### Weeks 1-2: platform contracts and boundaries

- Approve the platform constitution.
- Confirm canonical entity ownership by domain.
- Publish initial API and event naming rules.
- Define application, tenant, merchant, user and scope models.
- Establish platform decision records.
- Define plugin risk tiers and review workflow.
- Confirm corporate and IP registers even if one legal entity is retained.

### Weeks 3-4: application registry and access foundation

- Build internal application registration.
- Add service credentials and environment separation.
- Add merchant-authorised scopes.
- Add API gateway policies, quotas and audit.
- Implement signed webhooks and idempotency standards.
- Create sandbox fixtures from non-production data.

### Weeks 5-6: reusable service interfaces

- Expose organisation, branch, place, product, offer and search APIs to first-party applications.
- Expose media upload and moderation interfaces.
- Expose lead, request and outcome events.
- Implement common errors, pagination, filtering and version headers.
- Add the outbox pattern for reliable domain events.

### Weeks 7-8: first adapter framework

- Build payment-provider adapter contract.
- Build fulfilment-provider adapter contract.
- Build merchant feed and POS connector contract.
- Build notification-provider adapter contract.
- Create test providers for local development and acceptance testing.

### Weeks 9-10: first composed products

- Make Shoppage Merchant Sites consume the same public-domain services as Central and Showcase where practical.
- Implement Pemosites prototype as a first-party extension rather than a data fork.
- Implement one market-operator or distributor pilot using scoped APIs.
- Confirm that changes flow back through the canonical graph and audit system.

### Weeks 11-12: operational readiness

- Run security and privacy review.
- Test credential revocation, webhook replay, failed provider and data-export scenarios.
- Publish internal developer documentation and changelog.
- Establish plugin support and incident ownership.
- Measure core reuse, launch speed, error rate and data contribution.
- Approve the 4-6 month external beta cohort.

## 95.2 Month-3 functional acceptance criteria

The framework is considered functional when:

- all five public Shoppage layers authenticate through the common identity model;
- merchant, branch, product, offer and place records are reused rather than copied;
- at least three domain APIs are consumed by more than one application;
- signed webhooks and idempotent writes pass failure and replay tests;
- one payment and one fulfilment adapter contract work with test or approved partner endpoints;
- Pemosites or another extension renders a merchant experience from common data;
- a controlled external or separate-team application can operate with scoped credentials;
- all integration actions are attributable and auditable;
- access can be revoked without corrupting core data;
- data export and partner termination have tested procedures;
- the platform can show the cost and time saved through reuse.

## 95.3 Months 4-6: ecosystem expansion

- Launch a private developer portal beta.
- Onboard three to five certified integration partners.
- Publish TypeScript SDK and embeddable widgets.
- Pilot Pemosites with 10-20 merchants using the common graph.
- Pilot one market, procurement or distributor powered application.
- Launch plugin discovery and merchant consent screens.
- Add usage metering and partner billing.
- Add partner reliability, support and certification dashboards.
- Introduce privacy-safe aggregate intelligence APIs for approved use cases.
- Evaluate one vertical as a formal business line using the subsidiary admission tests.
- Establish standard platform-licence, data-processing, revenue-share and service-level templates.

## 95.4 What must not be attempted in months 0-6

- An unrestricted public app store with unreviewed code.
- Arbitrary third-party code running inside core production processes.
- Direct external database access.
- Dozens of speculative subsidiaries.
- A Shoppage-owned payment wallet or nationwide fleet.
- White-label forks with independent databases.
- Selling raw personal or merchant-confidential data.
- Premature microservice extraction solely to appear like a platform.
- Permanent custom integrations without shared contracts and support ownership.
- Claims of an open national platform before reliability, rights and governance are proven.

# 97. Investor interpretation and strategic value

The framework changes the investment case in five ways.

## 97.1 Lower marginal launch cost

Each new product can reuse identity, graph, search, trust, billing, AI, payments, fulfilment and analytics. Capital is directed to the differentiated customer problem rather than duplicated infrastructure.

## 97.2 Multiple revenue paths from one asset base

The same verified merchant, place, product and demand graph supports subscriptions, advertising, integrations, intelligence, websites, procurement, partner applications and selected transactions.

## 97.3 Option value without uncontrolled diversification

The venture-studio and subsidiary gates create strategic options while preserving focus. Weak ideas can be retired as modules. Strong businesses can receive dedicated leadership and capital after evidence.

## 97.4 Stronger data and distribution moat

Every productive extension can improve the graph, generate outcomes and increase the utility of Shoppage for all participants. This is harder to replicate than standalone marketplace traffic.

## 97.5 Clear asset ownership

A PlatformCo-style model protects core IP, schemas, contracts, evidence graph and APIs even if operating businesses change, partner or raise separate capital.

## 97.6 Investor risks to manage

- Platform ambition may outrun actual merchant and buyer adoption.
- Ecosystem governance may become bureaucratic.
- Subsidiaries may distract management or conceal weak economics.
- Partner integrations may create security and support liabilities.
- Internal products may unfairly compete with ecosystem partners.
- Revenue concentration may develop around one major institution or provider.
- Shared infrastructure costs may be misallocated.

Board reporting should therefore include core reuse, platform reliability, ecosystem revenue, subsidiary P&Ls, partner concentration, data-rights incidents and capital deployed by venture.

# 98. Final framework decision

The final position is:

> **Shoppage is both a national commerce product family and a reusable commerce framework. Central, Showcase, Ads, Creators and Markets are the flagship applications. The Shoppage Framework is the shared operating system beneath them. Approved subsidiaries, partners and independent companies may build specialised businesses on that framework through governed APIs, plugins, licences and shared services.**

The framework must remain practical:

- build the internal platform first;
- prove reuse across the existing five layers;
- use Pemosites as an early extension test;
- expose only stable, rights-cleared interfaces;
- add partners through a controlled beta;
- create subsidiaries only after product-market and organisational evidence;
- keep the core commerce graph, identity, trust, evidence and audit systems unified;
- allow innovation at the edge without fragmentation at the centre.

This structure turns Shoppage from a single application into foundational commercial infrastructure. It also creates a disciplined path for other Zimbabwean companies to innovate on local commerce data and distribution without rebuilding the national commerce layer or surrendering their own specialised business identity.
